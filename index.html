Virtual Standardized Patient Simulator

Practise a clinical consultation with a patient who lives on your own computer. It is free, and it works with the internet switched off.

1
Choose a patient
  • You get their age, complaint and vital signs.
  • The clock starts when you are ready.
2
Take the history
  • Ask what you would ask in clinic.
  • Press Examine to examine the patient.
  • Keep notes as you go.
3
Submit your diagnosis
  • Three diagnoses and three tests, against the clock.
  • Your answer is shown beside the case authors'.
Explore the technology behind it

Four parts

All open source. All free. All on your own computer.

The case files

Plain text, one per patient

The patient’s story, the vital signs, what the examination finds, and the answer key. Write your own and drop it in.

This program

Custom code, written with Claude Code

One web page that ties the other parts together. It runs the clock, shows the examination findings, passes your questions on, and grades your answer.

Ollama

Model manager

Downloads an open source AI model and runs it on your computer. No account. No key. No internet after the first download.

The model

qwen3:4b-instruct

A small open source AI model from Alibaba. 2.5 GB, downloaded once. It has one job here: it speaks as the patient.

Where the patient cases come from

They are published, peer reviewed teaching cases.

Adapted from Objective Structured Clinical Examination cases in MedEdPORTAL, the open access journal of the Association of American Medical Colleges.

  • Written by clinicians and reviewed before publication.
  • Each arrives with a patient script, a checklist, and the authors' own answer key.
  • The words the patient speaks are the authors' words.

Before you start

  • This is a demonstration of a teaching tool.
  • The patients are invented.
  • The model is small and can make mistakes while sounding certain.
  • Nothing here is medical advice. It is for education only.

2. Paste your source under it, and send

A published case, a teaching case from your school, or a patient you know well. If the patient is real, change the details so nobody can be recognised.

3. Save the answer

Save it as a text file in the cases folder, next to the ones that came with the program. Reload this page and it appears in the library.

4. Try it, then fix it

If a fact comes out that should have stayed hidden, add (only if asked) to that line. If a question is not counted, add another matching word. The file is plain English.

Keep the authors and licence of any published source in the [CREDIT] section.

MedEdPORTAL's Creative Commons Attribution condition

The cases come from MedEdPORTAL, the open access teaching journal of the Association of American Medical Colleges, published under the Creative Commons Attribution licence, written as CC BY.

CC BY lets you use, change and pass on the work, including commercially. In return it asks for three things:

  • Name the authors, with the title, the year and a link to the original.
  • Say what you changed.
  • Name the licence, so the next person knows their rights too.

How this program does that. The credit travels with the material, in five places: at the top of every case file with a list of changes, on the welcome screen, on the case card, on the result screen, and in every report written for a tutor.

If you pass this on or change a case, please keep that credit with it.
