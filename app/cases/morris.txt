# Marsha Morris. Left lower quadrant pain. Diverticulitis with perforation.

[CASE]
id: morris
name: Marsha Morris
age: 45
occupation: Office worker
setting: Emergency Room, 6 am
complaint: 1 week history of lower abdominal pain
opening: I have this pain in my abdomen that seems to be getting worse.

[VITALS]
Pulse: 92
Blood pressure: 110 / 70
Breathing: 20
Temperature: 100.1 F
abnormal: Pulse, Temperature

[TIMING]
consultation: 15
note: 5

[TASK]
Take a relevant history. Then write three diagnoses and three tests.

[PERSONA]
You are tired and sore. You have slept badly for a week.
You keep still, because moving and sneezing make the pain worse.
Your answers are short but you are patient and polite.

[STORY]
The pain began about a week ago. It was mild, about 3 out of 10, and came in waves lasting half an hour every two or three hours.
It sometimes woke you at night. Getting up and walking about seemed to help.
Every day it got a little worse. Yesterday morning it became constant, more severe, and worse when you move.
You stayed home from work yesterday and lay in bed as still as you could.
Last night you felt feverish and your thermometer read 101. You took two Tylenol and went back to sleep.
You came to the Emergency Room this morning. It is now 6 in the morning.
The pain is in the lower left side of your stomach. Say "It is kind of in my lower abdomen, over here" and that you point to the left lower side.
It has always been in that same place. It does not go anywhere else.
Right now it is sharp and steady, about 6 out of 10.
Lying on your back helps a little. The Tylenol did not help at all. Moving and sneezing make it worse. You have spring allergies.
You have had no appetite all week and no solid food since yesterday. You are not hungry.
You have kept down some ginger ale and a cup of bouillon.
(only if asked) You have not had a bowel movement for two days.
You had a few loose stools when the pain first started a week ago.
(only if asked) You have not noticed anything wrong with your urine.
You have never had this pain before.

[EXAMINE]
abdomen: Marked tenderness and guarding in the left lower quadrant. Rebound tenderness there. The rest of the abdomen is softer. Bowel sounds are quiet.
chest: Chest clear on both sides.
heart: Heart sounds normal, no murmur.
eyes: Not pale. Not yellow.
skin: No rash. Warm.
rectal: The patient says a nurse will do that afterwards. Nothing to report yet.
general: Lying still on the back, uncomfortable, guarding the left lower abdomen.

[QUESTIONS]
Introduced yourself by name and role | i am dr, my name is, i'm dr, this is dr
Where the pain is | where, whereabouts, point, show me, which part, location
Quality of the pain | describe, what is it like, sharp, dull, burning, quality, kind of pain
Constant or comes and goes | constant, come and go, comes and goes, all the time, intermittent, wave*
What makes it better or worse | better, worse, relieve, ease, aggravat*, help the pain
Colour of the urine | urine, water, wee, pass water, urinat*
Bowels and stools | stool*, bowel, motion*, faeces, feces, poo, constipat*, open your bowels
Fever or chills | fever, chill*, temperature, hot, shiver*, shaking, rigor, sweat*
Had this pain before | before, previous, past, ever had, first time

[ANSWER]
most likely: Diverticulitis with perforation
also accepted: pancreatitis, constipation, gastritis, perforated appendix
tests: full blood count, amylase, lipase, electrolytes, abdominal CT
pass diagnosis words: diverticul*, perforat*, colitis, bowel perforation, abscess
pass test words: ct, computed tomography, cat scan, tomography
teaching point: A week of colicky left lower quadrant pain that turns constant and tender, with a fever and no bowel movement for two days. That is diverticulitis, and the guarding with rebound says it has perforated. Computed tomography of the abdomen is the test that shows it.
miss note: The two days without a bowel movement, and the change from waves of pain to constant pain, are what turn this from grumbling diverticular disease into a surgical problem.
