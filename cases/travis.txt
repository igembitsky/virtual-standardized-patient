# Terri Travis. Sudden severe epigastric pain. Peritonitis from a perforated ulcer.

[CASE]
id: travis
name: Terri Travis
age: 60
occupation: Truck driver
setting: Emergency Room, 4 am
complaint: 6 hour history of severe upper abdominal pain
opening: I have this horrible pain in my stomach.

[VITALS]
Pulse: 110
Blood pressure: 100 / 60
Breathing: 24
Temperature: 101.4 F
abnormal: Pulse, Blood pressure, Breathing, Temperature

[TIMING]
consultation: 15
note: 5

[TASK]
Take a relevant history. Then write three diagnoses and three tests.

[PERSONA]
You are in severe pain, the worst you have known. You lie completely still because any movement hurts.
Your answers are very short. You cannot manage a long conversation.
You are frightened.

[STORY]
You were well until 10 o'clock last night, when the pain came on suddenly.
You thought it was indigestion and took several antacids, then went to bed.
The pain did not let up. At midnight you vomited. Vomiting did not change the pain. It just kept going.
(only if asked) The vomit was last night's dinner, greenish yellow, not much of it. No blood and nothing like coffee grounds.
You have no appetite. You have had nothing to eat or drink since the pain started.
It became so bad in the night that your wife brought you in. It is now 4 in the morning.
The pain is in the middle of your upper stomach, just below the breastbone. Say "It is mostly right here" and that you point there, and that now it hurts all over.
It was always in that same place at the start.
It also goes through to the middle of your back, like a knife.
It is 10 out of 10. Stabbing.
It never goes away. It is constant.
Nothing makes it better. Keeping completely still helps a little. Any movement makes it much worse.
(only if asked) You have had burning stomach pain on and off for months, and you take antacids most days.
(only if asked) You take ibuprofen most days for your back, from the driving.
You drove a three day route and got home last night.
You have not opened your bowels since yesterday.
(only if asked) Your stools have looked black once or twice in the last few weeks. You thought it was something you ate.
You smoke a pack a day. You drink a few beers most evenings.
You have never had pain like this before.

[EXAMINE]
abdomen: The whole abdomen is rigid and board like. Marked tenderness everywhere with rebound. Bowel sounds are absent.
chest: Chest clear on both sides.
heart: Heart sounds normal, fast, no murmur.
eyes: Not pale. Not yellow.
skin: Cool and clammy. No rash.
rectal: The patient says a nurse will do that afterwards. Nothing to report yet.
general: Lying rigidly still, knees drawn up, sweating, in severe pain.

[QUESTIONS]
Introduced yourself by name and role | i am dr, my name is, i'm dr, this is dr
Where the pain is | where, whereabouts, point, show me, which part, location
Quality of the pain | describe, what is it like, sharp, dull, burning, stabbing, quality, kind of pain
Constant or comes and goes | constant, come and go, comes and goes, all the time, intermittent
What makes it better or worse | better, worse, relieve, ease, aggravat*, help the pain
Colour of the stools | stool*, bowel, motion*, faeces, feces, poo, black, melaena, melena
Fever or chills | fever, chill*, temperature, hot, shiver*, shaking, rigor, sweat*
Had this pain before | before, previous, past, ever had, first time, indigestion, heartburn
Medicines including anti-inflammatories | medicine*, medication*, tablets, pills, drugs, ibuprofen, anti-inflammat*, painkiller*, antacid*

[ANSWER]
most likely: Peritonitis due to a perforated ulcer
also accepted: acute pancreatitis, gastritis, perforated appendix
tests: full blood count, amylase, lipase, electrolytes, upright chest X-ray, abdominal CT
pass diagnosis words: perforat*, ulcer, peritonitis, free air, viscus
pass test words: chest x-ray, chest xray, chest radiograph, erect film, upright, ct, computed tomography, tomography
teaching point: Pain that starts in a second, a board like abdomen with no bowel sounds, daily anti-inflammatories and months of indigestion. That is a perforated peptic ulcer with peritonitis. An upright chest film showing free air under the diaphragm makes the diagnosis at the bedside.
miss note: The daily ibuprofen and the months of burning indigestion are the history that explains the hole. Asking about medicines is what finds it.
